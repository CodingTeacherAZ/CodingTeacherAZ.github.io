Curriculum design
as systems work

Curriculum design at the systems level means more than writing lesson plans. It means building the architecture that holds an entire program together — the alignment between standards, instruction, and assessment; the vertical coherence across grade bands; the structures that make a course transferable, scalable, and rigorous.

Each project below represents a documented instance of that work — from a full grades 6–12 mathematics realignment to ground-up dual enrollment course design and a contemporary CTE software development curriculum.

Design Philosophy

Curriculum as Architecture

Design
Principles

Principle 01

Alignment is not a feature — it is the foundation.

Genuine vertical alignment means every standard, assessment, and instructional sequence is designed to build on what came before and prepare students for what comes next. When alignment is absent, rigor becomes inconsistent and students stall at transitions between courses or grade levels — not because they lack ability, but because the architecture failed them.

Principle 02

Assessment design comes first, not last.

Backward design — beginning with what mastery looks like and building instruction toward it — produces courses that actually measure the depth of thinking the standards demand. Using DOK and Bloom's Taxonomy at the design stage, not as a labeling exercise after the fact, keeps the cognitive demand honest.

Principle 03

Spiral review is architecture, not repetition.

Returning to prior concepts deliberately — at increasing depth and in new contexts — is structurally different from reteaching the same content. Built into the sequence from the start, spiral review produces long-term retention and prevents the skill erosion that typically accelerates by the end of a course.

Principle 04

Student-centered does not mean unstructured.

Asynchronous, differentiated learning requires more rigorous design than a lecture — not less. The structure has to live inside the materials: clear pathways, explicit success criteria, and scaffolding calibrated to where students actually are, not where the pacing guide assumes they should be.

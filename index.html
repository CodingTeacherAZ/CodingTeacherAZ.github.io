Designing and Scaling Instruction Across Career & Technical Education

This portfolio documents an approach to instruction grounded in three pillars: clarity of purpose, iterative design, and continuous alignment between standards, instruction, and assessment.

The work reflects a focus not only on effective classroom practice, but on making that practice visible, transferable, and scalable across contexts. Instruction is treated as a cycle—where learning goals are made explicit, student thinking is surfaced through structured practice, and instructional decisions are adjusted based on evidence of understanding.

Across artifacts, the emphasis is on reducing cognitive load through intentional design, strengthening alignment through Backward Design, and using data to inform responsive teaching. This approach supports both student learning and the development of instructional systems that other educators can implement and adapt.

Design Principles
Principle 01
Outcomes First

Instruction is grounded in clear outcomes. Units and lessons are designed using Backward Design to ensure alignment between standards, instruction, and assessment. This approach supports consistent expectations and coherent learning progression across classrooms, moving from foundational understanding to complex application.

Principle 02
Error as Evidence

Student errors are treated as actionable data. Patterns of misunderstanding are identified, analyzed, and used to inform instructional adjustments and targeted supports. Structured use of contrastive examples and error analysis builds student capacity for transfer while providing teachers with a common lens for diagnosing learning.

Principle 03
Present → Check → Adjust

Formative assessment is embedded throughout instruction. Student responses are used to identify trends, guide real-time adjustments, and inform subsequent planning. This continuous feedback cycle supports responsive teaching and can be applied consistently across classrooms to improve instructional decision-making.

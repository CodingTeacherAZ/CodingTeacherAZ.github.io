Primary Areas of Instructional Mastery

Competencies

1

Competency Area One

Structured Classroom Culture

Establishes highly structured classroom systems built on clear procedures, consistent routines, explicit academic and behavioral expectations, and predictable instructional workflows. Classroom traditions, defined performance standards, and continuous engagement structures create an environment where students work independently, collaborate productively, and remain academically engaged without idle time.

  • Establishes procedures and routines for all classroom operations
  • Creates classroom cultures centered on accountability, predictability, and productive independence
  • Combines high behavioral expectations with strong relational support
  • Designs self-paced environments with continuous student engagement
  • Maintains predictable routines that allow students to transition smoothly between independent, collaborative, and teacher-supported work.
2

Competency Area Two

Backward-Designed Curriculum Systems

Designs standards-aligned curriculum systems beginning with performance expectations and assessment targets rather than textbook sequencing. Instruction is organized into focused mastery-based lessons aligned to single measurement criteria, with scaffolded practice, spiraled review structures, and state-weighted assessment systems that continuously reinforce prior learning while preparing students for high-stakes examinations.

  • Designs lesson systems aligned to single measurement criteria and mastery progression
  • Engineers spiraled assessment systems using state-weighted domain item banks
  • Structures lessons for completion within a single class period while maintaining DOK/Bloom’s rigor
  • Integrates hands-on learning, multimodal instruction, reflective exits, and extension pathways into daily instruction
  • Continuously reinforces prior learning through cumulative assessment architecture
3

Competency Area Three

Structured Student Autonomy

Creates mastery-based learning environments that balance clear instructional expectations with meaningful student autonomy. Lessons are intentionally designed to function independently of continuous teacher-led instruction, allowing flexible pacing, individualized pathways, targeted intervention, and exploratory learning while ensuring all students continue progressing toward mastery.

  • Supports neurodiverse learners through multimodal and flexible instructional design
  • Adapts pacing, workload, and instructional pathways to student needs
  • Creates learning systems without ceilings for advanced learners
  • Frees instructional capacity for individualized support and intervention
  • Continuously refines instructional systems through reflection, observation, and student performance data
